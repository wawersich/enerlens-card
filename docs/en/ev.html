Wallbox and EV

A wallbox is a consumer like any other. The car’s state of charge comes from the car’s own integration, not from the wallbox - Home Assistant does not link the two, so the consumer is told which one belongs to it.

Try it: the wallbox charges for a minute and the ring around its icon fills up; then it pauses for half a minute, and the row shows the charge instead of the power. In Home Assistant a tap on the row shows power and charge over time.

This is the YAML:

consumers:
  - entity: sensor.wallbox_power
    name: Wallbox
    icon: mdi:ev-station
    position: top                            # fixed at the top (top | bottom)
    show: condition                          # keep it in the list: always | condition
    condition:                               # while the cable is plugged in
      entity: binary_sensor.car_charge_cable
      operator: "="
      value: "on"
    charge:
      soc: sensor.car_battery_level          # the car, in %

In the editor the consumer unfolds; under Display are Position in the list, Show in the list with the condition and, under State of charge, the State of charge entity (%):

A thin ring around the icon shows the charge. While the car charges, the row shows the power; otherwise it shows the charge instead, so the row needs no extra room. With the condition the row stays in the list as long as the car is plugged in - full and at 0 W too; more under [hold]. The condition can look at a wallbox status sensor as well: operator: "!=" with value: [available, none] means "plugged in, except in these states". When the car is asleep and its sensor unavailable, the last known charge is shown grey. Hover the icon for the value; a tap on the row opens a window with the power and the charge below it (see Energy); in the editor it is under the consumer, State of charge. It is not limited to cars - a power station works the same way, and so does a 3D printer with its print progress. "State of charge" does not fit there: under Label (in YAML charge.label) put e.g. "Print progress" instead - in the tooltip and in the detail window.

Pin, keep, highlight

This works for every consumer, not only the wallbox.

Position (position): top or bottom fixes the row at the top or bottom of the list; several stand in the order of the configuration, sorted by dragging in the editor. Without it the row sorts by power. The position only decides the place, not whether the row appears.

Show in the list (show): from its minimum power a consumer is listed anyway. always keeps it there at all times as well, condition only while the condition is met - at 0 W too. No setting hides a consumer that draws power.

Condition (condition): a sensor, an operator and a value. "=" and "!=" take one or more states ("is one of them" / "is none of them"), ">", "<", ">=" and "⇐" exactly one number, e.g. sensor.battery_voltage ">" 10.5. The operators are quoted in YAML. While the sensor is briefly unavailable, its last answer counts. For more - a range, an attribute, two conditions - build a helper in Home Assistant and use it as the sensor.

Maximum (max_consumers): held rows count and get their places first; the strongest consumers fill the rest. If more rows are held than the maximum allows, all held rows still appear - the editor then says that no other consumer will.

Highlight (highlight): bold: true sets name and value in bold, band: true lightly backs the row in the consumer’s colour; either on its own or both, and no extra room.

In the energy mode position and always apply the same way; a condition describes the state now and is not evaluated there.

Older spelling: charge.show with plugged and unplugged still works and means the same as show and condition. Changing the consumer in the editor writes it in the new form.
