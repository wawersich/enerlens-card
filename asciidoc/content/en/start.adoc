= EnerLens Card
:page-key: start
:nav-order: 1

A *highly configurable* energy flow card for Home Assistant. Solar, grid,
house and battery in four arrangements — plus, optionally, a breakdown of what
the house figure and the PV shares are actually made of.

The card here is not a recording but the real card with invented values – try
the switches above it and the quick buttons in its header.

== What it does differently

*Four arrangements.* Solar, grid, house and battery stand in a cross, in one of
two squares turned by 45° or in a triangle, with curved or straight lines. The
lists move to their nodes: with Square · solar left the PV rows stand left of
the drawing and the consumers right of it (see link:layout.html[Arrangements]).

*Consumers are optional.* If individual consumers are measured (a heat pump,
say, or a device behind a Shelly), their entities can be added. The list beside
the house node and the ring around it then show what makes up the house value.
The remainder holds everything that is not measured individually.

*Moving average.* A live power diagram jumps by kilowatts within seconds.
Alternatively the card shows moving averages — over 2 and 15 minutes by
default, both configurable; one chip in the header steps through _Now_,
_Ø 2 min_ and _Ø 15 min_. They calm the picture down without falsifying the
numbers. Clicking any element still opens Home Assistant's own dialog with the
real history; grid and battery with two sensors each show both directions
together in one window.

*Energy instead of power.* At the tap of a button the card shows the energy of
a period instead of the power: what came from the sun today, what the house used
this week and how much went to the grid. The periods are today, 24 h, week, 7
days, month and 30 days. No energy sensors needed: the card adds up the
statistics Home Assistant already keeps of the power sensors. Gaps in them are
filled and marked by colour. List and ring then show which consumer had how much
of it. Import and export, or charge and discharge, take one sensor per direction
– if you only have a signed one, a helper splits it (see link:energy.html[Energy over a
period]).

*Quick buttons in the header.* Up to four quick buttons switch the view with
one tap: now or average, power or energy, the PV scale, short or full consumer
list. Each one steps on with every tap or, expanded, shows all its values side
by side. For all of them together there are three styles: *Segment buttons*,
*Segment buttons compact* and *Single buttons*. Which values a quick button
offers is up to you; with a single value it disappears and the card shows the
same on every device. Every card remembers where it was left (see
link:quick.html[Quick buttons]).

*The consumer list sorts itself and glides.* Rows reorder by power on a fixed
beat and slide to their new position. A small filter button at the top shows
*every* consumer on request, regardless of power — for tapping the history of
a device that has just gone quiet.

*The ring follows along.* When the order changes, its segments travel to
their new places and overtake on an inner lane — to be seen in every card on
this page. With `ring.animation: fade` they keep their places and blend their
colours.

*The flow is yours to tune.* The dots show the power: the more flows, the
faster and the more of them. When they start, how fast they get and how many
there can be are all settings — one knob, `flow.peak_w`, for all of it, or
threshold by threshold. On top come six dot designs, a single dot colour for
the whole card, and idle lines that can be dimmed or hidden. All of it is in
the editor too.

*Extra values at the nodes.* Each circle can show a second value - in the
circle or right next to it, with a short prefix: today's yield beside the
solar power, the energy drawn from the grid today, a self-sufficiency rate at
the house, the battery's remaining energy. It can be any entity; the card
shows it the way Home Assistant does and computes nothing itself. A node's
name and state word can be hidden. See _Extra values at the nodes_.

*Narrow columns.* When there is no room beside the cross — on a phone or in a
narrow dashboard column — the consumer list moves below it. With
`list.always_below` it stays there on wide screens too.

*Set up in the editor.* Almost everything is a click in the GUI editor, with
the card live beside it — the colours too, with a picker that offers the ones
the card already uses.
