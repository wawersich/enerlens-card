= Animations
:page-key: animation
:nav-order: 10.5

The card moves two things: the consumer ring when the order of the consumers
changes, and the dots on the lines that show the flow. Both can be set. Under
_Flows_ → *Motion*, `off` stops every movement; the default `auto` follows the
device's "reduce motion" setting.

== The ring

The list sorts itself by power on a fixed beat, and the ring follows it. There
are three ways it can do that:

* *Overtake* (default): every segment travels to its new place. One that moves
forward dives to an inner lane, passes underneath the others and surfaces at
its place.
* *Cross-fade colours*: the places stay where they are, their colours blend
into the new order. Calmer, above all when the order changes often.
* *Jump*: no movement, the ring stands in the new order at once.

Try it: in this card four consumers trade places every four seconds, one
segment passing one, two or all three of the others. List and quick buttons are hidden, so that only the
ring is to be seen.

== The dots

The dots show where power flows. Their speed and number depend on the power:
below the *minimum power* (default 10 W) none runs, above it one slow dot
first; with more power it gets faster, then more join, until top speed and the
*maximum dots* are reached. Where that is, one value says: the *peak power of
the plant* (default 6000 W); for more control the single thresholds are under
*Fine-tune the motion*.

*Which line runs.* Only the nodes are measured; the card derives how the power
splits onto the lines. The default fits almost every installation; special
cases are at the end of the page under
<<battery-task,When battery and grid work against each other>>.

What a dot looks like is its *design*: *Plain dots* (default) are a circle with
nothing else, the others give it a bright core and, depending on the design, a
glow. The design changes only the look, never speed or number.

Try it: here all values stand still and nothing re-sorts - only the dots move.
The switches choose the design, the sliders the maximum number of dots (the
card takes 2 to 10, default 5), the dot size (40 to 150 %, default 100) and
the speed: seconds a run at the lower and
at the upper threshold, default 5 and 1.8. Below the lower threshold a dot
still slows a little with less power, about 5 % per halving, at most 30 % -
so several small sources do not run in lockstep.

[source,yaml]
----
ring:
  animation: fade          # overtake | fade | jump
flow:
  animation: auto          # auto | on | off
  design: neutrino
  max_dots: 8
  slow_s: 8                # seconds a run at the lower threshold
  fast_s: 3                # ... with a lot of power
----

== When battery and grid work against each other

The default fits almost every installation; this chapter matters only if your
battery has a different task. Which line runs is mostly clear. It is ambiguous
only when battery and grid work against each other:

* *A: battery discharges during export.*
* *B: battery charges during import.*

Which reading fits depends on the battery's task, set with
`flow.battery_task`:

* `zero_grid` (default): the battery is to keep the exchange with the grid at
  zero - the usual self-consumption, and arbitrage with a dynamic tariff too.
  PV supplies the house first; whatever the battery still trades with the grid
  runs between battery and grid: in A the battery feeds the grid, in B it
  charges from the grid.
* `supply_house`: the battery supplies the house and stores PV, PV and grid do
  the trading - useful with a high feed-in tariff, with "battery first", or for
  the same picture as Home Assistant's energy dashboard. In A the PV goes to the
  grid and the battery to the house, in B the PV charges the battery and the
  house draws from the grid.

This setting exists *in YAML only* - the editor has no field for it and leaves
it as it is:

[source,yaml]
----
flow:
  battery_task: supply_house   # zero_grid (default) | supply_house
----

Try it: the values stand still, PV covers the house each time.
