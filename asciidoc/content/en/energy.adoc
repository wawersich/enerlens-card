= Energy over a period
:page-key: energy
:nav-order: 9

Besides power, the card can show energy: what came from the sun today, what
was bought this week, what the heat pump used this month. It needs no energy
sensors for that - it adds up the long-term statistics Home Assistant keeps of
the power sensors that are already configured.

Try it: the card starts on *Today* and *% kWp*. The slider sets the time of
the invented day, and *Today* counts from midnight up to it. With *% kWp* the
PV ring shows the average power over the period against the peak power of all
sources, night hours included: it grows during the morning and shrinks again
in the evening, because the hours without sun count too. *100%* shows the
sources' shares instead, *kW* the power at this moment.

[[state-class]]
[IMPORTANT]
====
*Required: state class "Measurement".* Home Assistant keeps long-term
statistics only for sensors with the state class `measurement` ("Measurement"
in the UI). That applies to every power sensor of the card - solar, grid,
battery, house and consumers - and above all to helpers you create yourself:
with _Template → Sensor_ the field *State class* must be set to "Measurement".
Without it the energy mode shows "—" for that value; without it on solar, grid,
battery or house, the card offers no energy at all unless told to. A _sensor
group_ takes the state class of its members when they all have the same.

Statistics start only once the state class is set; set later, everything
before is missing. To check: _Developer tools → States_ - the entity must have
the attribute `state_class: measurement`.
====

[source,yaml]
----
view:
  energy_periods: [power, today, 24h, week, 7d, month, 30d]   # any of them
----

The quick button power/energy next to the filter button then steps through
_kW_ (the power view, `power`; _Watt_ with `power.unit: W`) and the periods
switched on. Without any setting it offers power, today, 24 h and week (see
_Quick buttons_):

[cols=",,",options="header",]
|===
|Key |Chip |Period
|`today` |Today |since midnight
|`24h` |24 h |the last 24 hours
|`week` |Week |since the first day of the week as set in your Home Assistant profile
|`7d` |7 days |the last 7 × 24 hours
|`month` |Month |since the first of the month
|`30d` |30 days |the last 30 × 24 hours
|===

Every period ends at the last statistics Home Assistant has written, a few
minutes behind the clock. The rolling ones are exact to five minutes; _30
days_ starts on the full hour, because Home Assistant keeps its five-minute
statistics only about ten days - up to an hour shorter, never made up.

* *PV and house* show one figure each, in kWh.
* *Grid* with two sensors shows the import above the icon (→) and the
export below it (←), *battery* with two sensors the discharge above (↑)
and the charge below (↓) - over a day, both happen. With one signed sensor
see below. The state of charge is a now, not a period, so it is not shown.
* *The list and the ring* work with the energy of the period. Thresholds
given in W count as W × 1 hour: `min_consumer_w: 10` hides consumers below
10 Wh.
* *The dots* run on the mean power over the period - its energy divided by
the time it took - with the usual thresholds, so a month moves like its
average day, not at top speed.
* Extra values and the state words ("import", "charging") are hidden; their
room stays, so nothing moves when you switch.
* Tapping a node still opens its history.

*One sensor or two for grid and battery.* An hourly mean of a signed sensor
cannot tell how much went each way - on most days the grid does both - only the
balance is exact. So with a single signed sensor the *grid* shows one figure,
its balance (→ net import, ← net export), which is also what its dots show, and
the *battery* shows no figure and no flow. PV, house and consumers are exact
either way. A _derived_ grid or battery (see above) does not work with the
energy mode: the option is locked in the editor and the quick button does not
appear. To see both directions, give the card two sensors each - two _Template →
Sensor_ helpers split a signed one (unit `W`, device class `power`, state class
`measurement` - see <<state-class,above>>):

[source,jinja]
----
{# import #}
{{ [states('sensor.grid_power')|float, 0]|max if has_value('sensor.grid_power') else none }}
{# export #}
{{ [-(states('sensor.grid_power')|float), 0]|max if has_value('sensor.grid_power') else none }}
----

The battery works the same way; positive means discharge there:

[source,jinja]
----
{# discharge #}
{{ [states('sensor.battery_power')|float, 0]|max if has_value('sensor.battery_power') else none }}
{# charge #}
{{ [-(states('sensor.battery_power')|float), 0]|max if has_value('sensor.battery_power') else none }}
----

If your sensor reports it the other way round (charging positive), swap the two
templates.

`none` while the sensor is unavailable keeps an outage out of the statistics
as a gap, rather than as a measured zero.

Statistics only exist from the moment a sensor is created, so new helpers show
"Month from 10/21" in the chip until a whole month has passed.

*How sure a figure is.* Home Assistant's statistics can have holes - a
restart, an integration that was down. The card fills an hour that is missing
for one of the balance values (PV, import, export, discharge, charge, house)
from the other five, otherwise from the same hour the day before (or after).
An hour without PV after a dark hour counts as zero: small inverters switch
off at night. A figure is then coloured by how much of the period was filled:

[cols=",",options="header",]
|===
|Filled |Shown
|up to 2 % |normal
|2 – 10 % |muted blue-grey
|more than 10 % |amber
|===

Hovering over the figure says how much. If one of the main sensors only has
statistics from a date inside the period, the whole period counts from then -
the chip says "Month from 10/8" in amber - so that the balance still adds up.
Nothing is ever extrapolated. A consumer that is newer than the period does not
shorten it; its row and the remainder are amber, and a consumer without
statistics (a sensor without a state class) is listed last with a dash.
