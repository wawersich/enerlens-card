= Colours and look
:page-key: look
:nav-order: 11

Every colour the card has sits under *Colours* in the editor: the seven nodes,
the extra values (one row per value set up), one row per consumer, and the stops
of the state-of-charge gradient. Each row shows the colour the card is painting
on the left and the configured value on the right — `Default` while none is set.


[.only-light]
image::editor-colors-en-light.png["The Colours group",490]

[.only-dark]
image::editor-colors-en-dark.png["The Colours group",490]

Clicking the swatch opens the picker: a saturation and brightness field over a
hue strip, with the value below it as hex or, switched over, as sliders for RGB
or HSL. Next to those sit the colours this card already uses — one click, and
two air conditioners get the same blue rather than two similar ones.

`CSS ▾` folds out a text field. That is the way in for anything that is not a
hex value: `var(--primary-color)` stays bound to the theme instead of being
frozen to a fixed value. That is why the field stayed — a picker on its own can
only do `++#++rrggbb`.

A consumer's colour therefore lives here, not on the consumer's own card. Two
places for one setting would be one too many.

The ends of the gradient are pinned: at 0% and 100% the colour can be changed,
the percentage cannot, and neither can be removed — the card refuses a gradient
that does not run the whole scale. Stops in between can be moved, added and
removed, as long as two remain.

Defaults come from Home Assistant's energy theme variables, so the card matches
the energy dashboard without configuration. To colour by good and bad instead:

[source,yaml]
----
colors:
  grid_export: "#43a047"        # exporting is good
  grid_import: "#e53935"        # importing is not
  battery_charge: "#43a047"
  battery_discharge: "#e53935"
----

The arrows at grid and battery are red and green on their own: red for import
and discharge, green for export and charge, in the theme's colours
(`--error-color`, `--success-color`). To have them in their direction's colour
instead, untick *Colours → Arrows*, in YAML `colors.arrows: direction`.

== Transparent background (card-mod)

To let a dashboard wallpaper show through, the card can be made transparent
with https://github.com/thomasloven/lovelace-card-mod[card-mod] (card version
0.8.2 or later):

[source,yaml]
----
card_mod:
  style: |
    ha-card {
      --ha-card-background: transparent;
      background: none;
      box-shadow: none;
      border: none;
    }
----

The first line is the one that matters here: the four nodes are filled with the
card's background colour, and `background: none` on its own leaves them
standing as solid discs. This works with `appearance: auto` only - set to
`light` or `dark`, the card paints its own background, and card-mod does not
reach it.
